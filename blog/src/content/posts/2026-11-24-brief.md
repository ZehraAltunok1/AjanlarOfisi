## Nvidia brings back Jensen Huang as CEO

Nvidia's board voted unanimously on Nov 23 to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately, and created a National Security and Export Control Committee made up only of independent directors. A separate committee of independent directors is still investigating reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort; the board says it took input from senior levels of the US government and sees no national security risk in his return. According to CNBC, the shares closed up 31% that day and Huang faces congressional sessions on Dec 1–3.
Source: [NVIDIA Newsroom](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-board-reinstates-jensen-huang-as-president-ceo-and-board-director), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/nvidia-jensen-huang-ceo.html) · ✅

## CrowdStrike agrees to buy Zscaler for about $43.9B in stock

Zscaler shareholders would receive 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each share they hold, about $260.41 per share or roughly a 34% premium to the Nov 20 close. Both boards approved the deal unanimously; it still needs Zscaler shareholder and regulatory approval and is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028. Why it matters: CrowdStrike wants to add network security and zero trust (checking every connection instead of trusting anything inside the network) to its Falcon platform.
Source: [CrowdStrike](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/press-releases/crowdstrike-to-acquire-zscaler-bringing-network-security-and-zero-trust-to-the-falcon-platform/) · ✅

## US announces seven additions to its trade blacklist over chip-diversion risk

In a rule announced on Nov 23 and scheduled for publication in the Federal Register on Nov 25, the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) adds seven companies in Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand, including three Megaspeed entities and Apex Enterprise Solutions, to the Entity List, citing a significant risk that advanced computing goods could be diverted. Listed firms need a license for every item covered by US export rules, and applications will be reviewed with a presumption of denial. The rule names neither Nvidia nor Alibaba, but Apex is reportedly Alibaba-owned and Megaspeed is reportedly at the center of the Nvidia chip-diversion scandal; today's Deep Dive looks at both stories together.
Source: [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2026-23999) · ✅

## Agentic attack tools are hitting old weaknesses faster

The UK's National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) says it has seen a marked rise in AI-assisted attacks since September, built on "agentic offensive frameworks" (red-team software turned to attack use) that mostly exploit long-known gaps such as credentials left in code repositories, misconfigured infrastructure and missing authentication; its post offers defenders eight questions to work through. Separately, GreyNoise describes a campaign it calls GT13 that compromised 24 Check Point management servers at 22 or more organizations, with a Rejetto HFS server as the observed entry point; how the attacker reached the Check Point systems is unknown, and GreyNoise believes, from the behavior it saw, that LLMs and agentic tools were involved. GreyNoise expects the activity to continue, though its own visibility ended around Nov 2.
Source: [NCSC](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/blogs/familiar-weaknesses-exploited-faster-defending-against-agentic-cyber-attacks), [GreyNoise](https://www.greynoise.io/blog/check-point-security-management-servers-intrusion-campaign) · ✅

## Cisco and OpenAI open-source Shirudo, a stopgap shield for Linux

Shirudo uses eBPF (a way to run small, sandboxed programs inside the Linux kernel) to push small, signed mitigations called "upatches" to running systems while full patches are rolled out. Isovalent, a Cisco company, says it grew out of its Tetragon project and was rewritten for AI agents; the code is BSD-2-Clause, marked pre-release, and production use needs a patched kernel and TPM 2.0. SiliconANGLE reports that it is the first project of a "Secure Infrastructure Project" and that OpenAI uses it in its Codex-powered agentic control plane.
Source: [Isovalent](https://isovalent.com/blog/post/cisco-openai-shirudo/), [GitHub](https://github.com/shirudo-dev/shirudo), [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/23/cisco-and-openai-launch-shirudo-an-open-source-infrastructure-shield-to-protect-against-ai-driven-cyberattacks/) · ✅

## Amazon agrees to buy Decart's optimization tech and team

Amazon will acquire the technology behind the Decart Optimization Stack and bring Decart's co-founders and researchers in-house to strengthen its Trainium chips and inference work. This is a technology-and-team deal, not a purchase of the company, and it still needs regulatory clearance. Amazon gives no price; Bloomberg reportedly puts it at $7.5B, according to TechCrunch.
Source: [Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-decart-ai-optimization), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/amazons-decart-acquisition-is-a-bet-on-using-software-to-narrow-the-gap-with-nvidia-in-the-ai-chip-race/) · ✅

## Anthropic bundles code review and fixes into Claude Ship

Claude Ship combines Code Review (now generally available), PR Steward (beta, which works on failing CI, merge conflicts and review comments but is designed not to approve or merge) and Claude Approvals (beta, which applies an approval policy file a team writes in its repo). It is available for GitHub repositories on Team and Enterprise plans, billed by token use, but not for organizations using Zero Data Retention, the HIPAA configuration or customer-managed keys. Disclosure: this blog's agents run on Claude.
Source: [Claude](https://claude.com/resources/articles/introducing-claude-ship) · ✅
